While browsing web design review posts on reddit, I came across one whose designer made a shocking decision to include a total lockout about 30 seconds in. Meaning: after 30 seconds, the site locks you out and *requires* you create an account to continue browsing. This is a car research and comparison site. The whole point of a site like this is to get people to stay on your site for as long as possible, so when it locked me out, my jaw dropped. I opened up an incognito window just to check if it was a glitch or a feature. It was a feature.

I replied to the original poster with one sentence, something to the effect of “kill the account lockout so I can do more.” 

Locking out does work in certain situations, but not this one. Lockouts are useful when the content is truly valuable or unique. Car comparisons are not unique. My guess is the lockout idea was generated by an AI coding assistant which the website creator blindly agreed to. They probably thought this was a “pro” feature and that it would improve the value. I believe if they left that feature on, the site would go nowhere forever.

The skill of knowing when to use features like a lockout is the very definition of expertise in user experience and is worth real money. This reddit website review is a clear example of how UX and UI expertise is quickly going to become a premium service in this era of AI-generated web design.

## The Website Was “Real”

The site that I audited was real, real in that it was deployed on a real domain, responds to interaction, holds together structurally and overall reads and works properly. 3 to 4 years ago that would have been a several thousand dollar job at minimum, especially with the database on car brands and models this one has. Today, it's a several minute job. Today, the generated website is generally correct. The job of creating a website that people can compare and browse car features was performed, by a machine, with I’m guessing no budget needed.

This is a signal that web industry professionals whose job is assembling a template should be worried. That's coming from a place of compassion and concern, not pointing a finger or subjugation. I myself built every site with WordPress x Avada since 2017, but in the last 6 months of getting my hands on Claude Code, I was able to forgo both entirely. 

During the first several months of getting acquainted with Claude Code, I realized that the middle was dying. What I didn’t realize was just how fast it was going to die, because, by golly, it’s nearly there.

## The Middle is Dead

So “the middle is dead,” but what does that actually mean? For the entire history of the internet up until 2026, the bulk of the work and skill involved in creating a website was the design and development of the website: the code behind the website and the interface, graphics and copy that goes on top of it. Together, the design and the development create the interactive entity we call *a website*. 

Before AI, that design and development generally took up the lion’s share of time and budget for most non-enterprise or complex ecom/publishing website projects. User experience and user interface weren’t typically sold as a line item; they were rolled into design and development because any decent web designer naturally uses UI and UX skills while they're developing a site. (Mind you, UI and UX skills were previously at a premium during the early days of interface design, when we were—for the first time—figuring out how to literally in-ter-face with a computer, so this is coming full circle.)

What killed the middle was AI taking the bulk of the work and turning it into the smallest part of the work. Now, you can type in a few sentences to an AI coding agent and get a fully blown website that has been “designed” and “developed” in a matter of minutes. When something like this happens—when supply goes vertical and demand essentially stays the same—the price of that thing going vertical (passable design and development) goes to zero. However, the price of whatever was orbiting that now-commodity becomes a premium. 

Website design was almost always human judgment bundled with hard code and production. That bundle has now come apart. Design and development is now a Red Lobster level commodity, while excellent user experience and polished user interface are becoming Michelin star restaurants. With AI, everybody can build a Red Lobster, but most businesses, brands and organizations don’t want a commodified chain restaurant, they want a Michelin star restaurant, or at the very least, their own independent and unique establishment.

## The Shift To Premium

I didn't wake up one day, read the doom on LinkedIn and make the reactive decision to charge more for my services. No. I realized that the sailboat middle was sinking and I was not going to go slide down to the commodified low-end dingy. I’m guessing that low end is going to bottom out completely in the next few years as AI does what it does and gets better, faster. 

The premium that I speak of is not something materializing from ego or economic *fear,* but rather economic awareness and knowing my value. Anyone that has a UI/UX skill not yet available to AI models should be charging a premium for it. The decoupling of UI/UX from web design is the argument. 

**This is a seismic shift in the web design market.** The sale is no longer the website; the sale is the skill to turn a Ruby Tuesday’s into 11 Madison Park. 

Instead of charging $10,000 for a website that *includes* premium UI/UX and takes a whole month, we might now charge $1,000 \- $2,500 for the UI/UX layer itself, taking only a matter of hours or days to complete.  
Instead of building one now-commodified site a month for $10,000, we could be performing 10 AI transformation audits per month at $2,000 a pop. This could be a windfall for those that position themselves strategically. 

## This Is Not Extraction

If you read my work here on chadworks, or on [chadlewine.com](http://chadlewine.com) or on [illuscaden.com](http://illuscaden.com), you will see that I am vehemently anti-extraction. I don’t see this shift-to-premium as an opportunity to extract more money from Main St, no, I see this as half of my business is gone, so how am I going to compensate for that part of my income so I can literally stay alive and survive in this economy? The answer was to repackage my services and reposition my brand as a necessary premium, with proof behind it.

The repricing and premium valuation I'm placing on UI and UX skills is a result of three things compressing at once:

1. **The cost of producing a passable website has dropped to essentially zero** plus an AI coding assistant subscription. Launching your own passable website is as relatively cheap and easy as dialing a number into a vending machine.  
2. **The sheer number of websites being produced and deployed**. All industries and at all levels within each industry’s hierarchy are being flooded with AI generated landing pages, microsites and apps, saying nothing of the AI generated graphics (slop) flooding feeds and inboxes everywhere. This echoes what is happening in the music industry—the sheer number of AI generated songs is, by nature, altering the market and value of products within it.  
3. **The consumer’s ability to recognize an AI generated entity on sight, and what they do with that judgement.** The consequence of this is that looking AI generated now carries a cost that it did not carry just a year or even 6 months ago. A site can be penalized by both the human consumer unconsciously or consciously for looking exactly like something that was passable and even possibly admired pre-2026. 

## AI-Generated Websites are Making UI and UX Expertise A Real Premium, Real Fast

Due to the commodification of website design and development—the actual code, layout and text—what has not yet been commodified should be priced at a premium. User experience and user interface expertise is going to be necessary to transform these commodified sites from a slop commodity into a distinguished and usable digital destination. Anybody that wants to stand out online, what they're going to be paying for is the human eye and understanding of the human brain. This is what I believe will be rapidly repriced at a premium we haven’t seen before.
